# M15 — Native Evidence Provenance & Claim Verification

**Status:** Initial native engine integrated into the Rust runtime  
**Engine ID:** `engine.vrtc.evidence`  
**Capability:** `evidence.verify`

## Research basis

M15 adopts two standards-oriented data-model patterns:

- **W3C PROV-O:** separates entities, activities, and agents, and models how source material is used or generated by activities and attributed to responsible agents. VRTc represents source material, retrieval provenance, and explicit assessors as distinct fields rather than flattening them into an opaque confidence score.
- **W3C Web Annotation Data Model:** treats annotations as relationships between bodies and targets, and supports references to specific resource segments. VRTc's initial record stores an excerpt as the evidence body and a canonical source URL as its target. Text-fragment selectors and richer annotation serialization remain future work.

These standards inform VRTc's contracts; this crate does not require an RDF store or annotation server.

## Native data model

### Claim
- Stable claim ID.
- Claim statement.
- Creation timestamp.

### Evidence record
- Stable evidence ID and linked claim ID.
- Source/provider/crawler identifier.
- Canonical HTTP(S) source URL and derived source host.
- Source title and bounded excerpt.
- Caller-supplied 64-character SHA-256 content fingerprint.
- Retrieval timestamp.
- Extraction confidence supplied by the upstream extraction pipeline.

### Assessment
- Evidence relation: `Supports`, `Contradicts`, `Context`, or `Unclear`.
- Confidence in the assessment, bounded to [0, 1].
- Assessor ID and method: human, rule-based, model-assisted, or external system.
- Rationale and assessment timestamp.

Only explicit assessments are recorded. The engine does not infer semantic entailment from a URL, excerpt, title, source rank, or hash.

## Verification lifecycle

```text
Create claim
    |
Attach source evidence
    |
Explicit assessment per evidence record
    |
Aggregate evidence pool
    |
    +-- no evidence -----------------> InsufficientEvidence
    +-- unassessed evidence ---------> NeedsReview
    +-- support + contradiction -----> Mixed
    +-- enough supporting hosts -----> Supported
    +-- enough contradicting hosts --> Contradicted
    +-- otherwise -------------------> NeedsReview
```

## Status semantics

- **InsufficientEvidence:** no evidence records are attached.
- **NeedsReview:** evidence is unassessed, only contextual/unclear, has too few distinct hosts, or does not meet the configured confidence threshold.
- **Supported:** all attached evidence is assessed, supporting evidence meets the distinct-host threshold, and mean supporting assessment confidence meets the configured threshold.
- **Contradicted:** all attached evidence is assessed, contradicting evidence meets the distinct-host threshold, and mean contradicting assessment confidence meets the configured threshold.
- **Mixed:** the assessed pool contains both supporting and contradicting evidence.

These are summaries of the recorded evidence pool, not declarations of objective truth. In particular, `Contradicted` means the current pool leans against the claim under the configured rule; it is not proof that the claim is false.

## Source diversity and confidence limitations

- The initial independence heuristic counts distinct hostnames, not independent publishers or ownership groups. Subdomains may belong to the same organization, and unrelated sites may republish one source.
- Mean assessment confidence is a descriptive aggregation, not a calibrated probability.
- Extraction confidence is retained separately from assessment confidence.
- The content hash is accepted as caller-supplied provenance metadata after format validation. The engine does not possess the full source bytes and therefore does not independently recompute that hash.
- A source URL and excerpt do not establish source authority or truth.
- Human, model-assisted, and external assessment methods are recorded as caller declarations; the engine does not authenticate those identities or methods.

## Resource and security limits

- Configurable maximum claim count, evidence count, evidence per claim, statement bytes, excerpt bytes, and rationale bytes.
- Assessment confidence and extraction confidence must be finite values in [0, 1].
- Evidence URLs must be absolute, credential-free HTTP(S) URLs.
- Source content fingerprints must be 64 hexadecimal characters.
- Evidence and assessment IDs cannot be silently overwritten.
- Deployments must authenticate assessors at the API host and use pseudonymous assessor IDs. Do not put personal information in rationales.

## Runtime integration

The runtime statically registers `engine.vrtc.evidence` / `evidence.verify` and exposes `evidence_engine()`. It is separate from Search, Metasearch, Retrieval, and Human Feedback so provenance, relevance judgments, and claim assessments do not become conflated.

## Boundaries

- Initial evidence and assessment storage is in-memory and bounded.
- No RDF serialization, PROV-O export, Web Annotation JSON-LD export, durable ledger, digital signatures, or append-only audit store yet.
- No automated fact-checking model or semantic entailment engine.
- No automatic trust score for publishers.
- No source-fetching behavior; evidence arrives from upstream Search, Crawler, or caller-provided sources.
